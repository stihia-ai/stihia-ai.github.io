# Design Context

## Users

- **Audience**: CEOs and founders of software development services SMEs (10–500 employees) in CEE, with Bulgaria as the core market. Their companies are transitioning to an AI-first SDLC.
- **Context**: AI tools spread through their teams bottom-up — assistants, coding agents, personal accounts — faster than any rules. Clients are starting to ask how their data is protected, and EU regulation (EU AI Act, NIS2, DORA) is approaching. Confusion is high and internal AI security expertise is low. Most visitors arrive through a referral from the network, so the site's job is to confirm credibility and make the offering concrete.
- **Job to be done**: Understand within minutes whether Stihia can take the company from scattered AI use to governed, AI-first delivery — employee education, internal policies, implementation, centralized monitoring, shadow AI detection, and customer-visible proof — and decide to book a conversation. Procurement is lean; the CEO can decide quickly.
- **Emotional goal**: Relief and orientation: "This is manageable with the right partner." Avoid urgency, hype, and fear.

### CEO Persona

- Tech-savvy founder or CEO, often an ex-engineer. Pragmatic, hands-on, wears many hats.
- Pro-AI: pushes AI-first delivery for competitiveness, and knows uncontrolled use can destroy the client trust the business runs on.
- No CISO and no dedicated security function; compliance expertise in-house is thin.
- **Pain**: Scattered AI usage across the org, spreading shadow AI, no clear rules or boundaries for employees, and regulations they have not had time to decode.
- **Fear**: Client data leaking through an AI tool — and losing deals because they cannot demonstrate AI security maturity.
- **Need**: One partner covering education, policy, implementation, and monitoring, without hiring a full-time officer. Proof of AI security they can show their customers.
- **Skepticism**: Allergic to consultant fluff and AI hype. "Is this a PDF nobody reads, or will it change how my team actually works?" Wants concrete deliverables, working tools, and plain answers.

## Brand Personality

- **Three words**: Clear. Credible. Pragmatic.
- **Voice**: A knowledgeable guide, not a vendor. Plain language first; regulatory and security terms are explained, never assumed. Technical depth is available — the audience is technical — but jargon never carries the message.
- **Tone**: Calm authority. Reassuring without minimizing the problem. Never sells through fear.
- **Copy**: Lead with the CEO's outcomes — control, compliance, employee efficiency, customer trust. Name concrete deliverables, tools, and frameworks instead of superlatives. AI-positive throughout: the message is "more AI, safely," never "less AI."

## Aesthetic Direction

- **Theme**: Dark, warm, restrained. Expert and approachable — not a war room, not a corporate consultancy. Premium enough to justify expert fees, welcoming enough for a first conversation.
- **Imagery**: Clean diagrams for the engagement journey and the policy-to-framework mapping. No stock consulting photography.
- **Density**: Spacious and easy to scan. The offering is broad and must read as one coherent journey, never a menu wall.

### Color Palette

Warm-dark foundation with a rare cool counterpoint.

- **Surfaces**: Layered warm darks — near-black base through warm ash panels. Each elevation is a subtle step, never a hard jump. All surfaces carry a faint warm undertone.
- **Text**: Warm gray ramp from bright off-white down to muted ash. Grays are always warm-tinted, never cool or blue.
- **Warm accents**: Amber, gold, and copper (OKLCH hue range ~48–88). The dominant color voice — journey stages, content emphasis, and visual rhythm.
- **Cool accent**: Muted steel blue (OKLCH hue ~240–250, restrained chroma). Reserved for rare moments where the design needs a deliberate temperature break — an interactive state, a focused highlight, or a single artifact that must stand apart from the warm field. No more than 5–10% of the color surface.
- **CTAs**: Inverted — light text on the dark field. No colored CTA buttons.
- **Borders**: Opacity-based warm tints. Separate without competing.

The warm accents lead; the cool accent punctuates. No pure black or pure white — all extremes are warm-shifted. Journey-stage colors remain in the amber-to-gold family and do not use the cool accent.

## Anti-References

- **No purple gradients.** Banned.
- **No consumer/startup vibes.** Avoid playful illustrations, emoji copy, candy gradients, or "fun" design language.
- **No cybersecurity clichés.** Avoid neon green, matrix rain, skulls, hacker imagery, padlock clipart, and dark-web aesthetics.
- **No consultancy clichés.** Avoid stock handshakes, suits at whiteboards, shield-and-checkmark clipart, and gavel-and-paperwork imagery.
- **No fear-selling.** Avoid breach headlines, red alert dashboards, and scare statistics.

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity at scan speed.** The core value must be visible even at thumbnail size. Busy CEOs decide in seconds.
2. **Make the path visible.** Present the portfolio as one journey — educate, govern, prove — so every offering reads as a step toward control, not an item on a menu.
3. **Restrained contrast, clean hierarchy.** Use type scale, spacing, and subtle borders, not loud color, to guide attention.
4. **Structure over spectacle.** Prefer soft panel separation, hairline connectors, and deliberate whitespace. The feel should be engineered, not decorated.
5. **Minimal text in visuals.** Diagrams and screenshots should explain themselves.
6. **Trust through restraint.** Sophistication comes from what is left out. Every element must earn its place.
7. **Proof, not promises.** Show policy structure, workshop outlines, and the badge. Let concrete deliverables create credibility.

## Technical Foundation

- **Typography**: Space Grotesk for body and UI, Goldman only for the "STIHIA" wordmark, and system monospace for code. Use fluid `clamp()` sizing for responsive headings.
- **Color**: Warm layered dark backgrounds with warm gray text. Accents in OKLCH amber, copper, and gold (hue ~48–88) keyed to journey stages and content emphasis. Steel blue cool accent (hue ~240–250) for rare highlights. Opacity-based borders. Tokens live in `assets/css/style.css`.
- **Layout**: Bootstrap 5.3 grid, generous section spacing, rounded cards, and constrained content widths.
- **Motion**: Subtle scroll-triggered fade-ins and card hover lifts. Use progressive enhancement so content is visible by default and animation is added through JS. Support `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Accessibility**: Target WCAG AA. Use semantic HTML, ARIA labels, keyboard navigation, and warm-on-dark contrast instead of harsh pure white on pure black.

## Product Context

A combined offering that takes an SME from scattered AI use to governed, AI-first delivery. Three stages:

1. **Educate** — Corporate workshops on AI security risks and mitigations, and on effective and secure AI use.
2. **Govern** — Preparation of internal AI policies in accordance with the EU AI Act, NIST, NIS2, DORA, ISO 27001, SOC 2, and ISO 42001; AI policy implementation consulting; and a fractional AI compliance and security officer for ongoing ownership.

Other products, outside of the core journey:

* **Stihia Sense** — Real-time AI threat detection with three detection layers (deterministic, probabilistic, anomaly), a web console with dashboards, two integration paths (SDK for custom AI; daemon plus API for well-known tools), real-time alerts, SIEM support through OpenTelemetry, and flexible deployment (cloud, isolated, self-hosted).
* **Stihia Zmey** — An interactive game that teaches AI agent vulnerabilities through prompt techniques. It serves as a marketing and awareness tool, and as a credibility signal.

### Key Messages

- **One partner from AI confusion to AI control.** Workshops, policies, implementation consulting, and a fractional AI compliance and security officer cover the whole path — no full-time hire required.
- **Compliance made concrete.** Policies aligned with the EU AI Act, NIST, NIS2, DORA, ISO 27001, SOC 2, and ISO 42001 — with clear internal accountability that gives the company legal safety.
- **More AI, safely — and trust customers can see.** Employees learn to use AI effectively and securely, and the Secure AI Deployer badge turns that discipline into a customer-visible signal.
