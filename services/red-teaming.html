What we attack

Six attack surfaces covering how AI products actually get broken.

  • Prompt injection & jailbreaks - one-shot and multi-turn; direct and indirect injection via retrieved content and tools.
  • System prompt & sensitive data leakage - coaxing the model to reveal instructions, PII, credentials, financials, and proprietary data.
  • Agent abuse - tool misuse, memory and context poisoning, goal hijack, privilege escalation, unsafe code execution, cross-tenant contamination.
  • RAG & knowledge poisoning - malicious documents in the index, retrieval-time injection, source-attribution integrity, confident hallucinations drawing on poisoned grounding.
  • Evasion & prompt brittleness - repeat trials, perturbation, stochastic-output variability; prompts that fail once and succeed later.
  • Resource & cost exhaustion - DoS, token thrash, quota drain, rate-limit bypass, denial-of-wallet attacks.

How an engagement runs

Four steps from scope to verified fix.

01

Scope

Agree system boundary, threat model, attack goals, scope and out-of-scope, and data-handling rules.

02

Attack

Manual and automated probing aligned to the risk taxonomy; multi-turn; realistic attacker scenarios.

03

Report

Every finding documented with reproduction steps, severity rating, impact, and ranked remediation.

04

Re-test

Return after remediation to verify fixes; lightweight follow-up engagement.

The findings report

Every finding reproducible, severity rated (CRITICAL / HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW), with concrete fixes and an attacker narrative. Maps to what EU AI Act and ISO 42001 expect from red-team exercises and gives clients doing security reviews an evidence trail.
